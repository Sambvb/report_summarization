Northgate Retail Group plc - Annual Results for the Fiscal Year Ended 31 March 2025 (fictional company, for demo purposes)

Northgate Retail Group plc reports a challenging year marked by weak consumer demand, elevated input costs and significant one-off charges. Group revenue declined 7% YoY to $4.6 bn, while like-for-like sales across the store estate fell 9%, reflecting lower footfall and a sharp reduction in discretionary spending by customers as inflation squeezed household budgets.

Gross margin contracted by 340 bps to 29.1%, as the group increased promotional activity and markdowns to clear excess inventory accumulated during the first half of the year. Cost of goods sold was further impacted by higher freight and energy costs. Operating expenses rose 4% despite the decline in sales, largely due to wage increases and higher rent on renewed store leases.

As a result, operating profit before exceptional items fell 62% to $96 mn and the operating margin narrowed to 2.1% from 5.2% in the prior year. EBITDA declined 38% to $310 mn.

The group recorded exceptional charges of $420 mn during the year. These include an impairment of $260 mn against the carrying value of 85 underperforming stores, a write-down of $70 mn on obsolete inventory, and restructuring costs of $90 mn related to the closure of the online home furnishings division and the associated reduction in headcount of approximately 1,800 roles. Consequently, the group reported a net loss of $395 mn, compared with a net profit of $118 mn in FY2024. Basic earnings per share were negative at -$0.92.

Cash flow from operations weakened considerably. Free cash flow was negative $145 mn, compared with positive $210 mn last year, driven by lower profitability and an outflow in working capital. Net debt increased to $1.9 bn from $1.4 bn, and leverage (net debt to EBITDA) rose to 6.1 times, well above the group's target range of 2.0 to 2.5 times.

The group's liquidity position has tightened. Following the deterioration in earnings, Northgate was at risk of breaching a financial covenant on its revolving credit facility. In March 2025, the group agreed a temporary covenant waiver with its lending banks, which will remain in place until September 2025. Shortly afterwards, two credit rating agencies downgraded the company's credit rating to below investment grade, which is expected to increase future borrowing costs. Interest expense for the year rose 31% to $112 mn.

In light of the losses and the need to protect liquidity, the Board has decided to suspend the final dividend. No dividend will be paid for the fiscal year. The previously announced share buyback programme has also been cancelled.

Management highlighted a small number of positive developments. The beauty and personal care category grew sales by 6%, and the loyalty programme added 1.2 million new members. The group also completed the sale of a non-core distribution centre for $65 mn, with the proceeds used to reduce debt.

The group has launched a three-year turnaround plan focused on reducing costs by $200 mn, closing a further 40 loss-making stores, and refinancing the bonds that mature in 2026. However, management cautioned that refinancing may be difficult on acceptable terms given current market volatility and the lower credit rating.

Outlook: trading conditions are expected to remain difficult. The group does not expect a recovery in like-for-like sales during the first half of FY2026 and has withdrawn its medium-term guidance. Market share losses to discount competitors and online-only retailers are expected to continue. The Chief Financial Officer stated: "This was a very disappointing year. Our priority now is to stabilise the business, protect cash and strengthen the balance sheet. There remains material uncertainty about the timing of a recovery."
